Architecture — how it mirrors StrategyArena

StrategyArena isn't one file — it's a platform: an LMS shell wired to a live Supabase backend, a separate digital-textbook file, and one interactive game file per topic. Economics & Film reproduces that structure exactly, so the two courses look and behave identically and share the same login, gradebook, and grading engine.

The pattern we're copying

StrategyArena today

  • index.html — the shell: auth, dashboard, textbook launcher, arenas, practice, exams, assignments, gradebook, certificates.
  • strategyarena-curriculum.html — the 14-chapter digital textbook, opened per chapter via #chapter-N anchors.
  • strategyarena-<slug>.html ×13 — one interactive arena per topic.
  • Supabase project qgelvmefyexyzpwqupev — ~30 sa_* server functions handle grading, so quiz/exam answers never reach the browser.
What we build

Economics & Film

  • econfilm-index.html — the same shell, rebranded, pointed at a new course row, with the film curriculum wired in.
  • econfilm-curriculum.html — the new 13⁠+⁠1-chapter textbook (the "very interesting read").
  • econfilm-<slug>.html ×13 — one arena per film/topic.
  • Same Supabase — a new course added to the existing platform: shared auth & gradebook, its own question banks, exam, papers, and arena slugs.
Why "same backend" is the right call

The shell is already multi-course (course picker, join codes, per-course weights, subscriptions). Adding Economics & Film as a new course means students use one account across both courses, you grade from one instructor console, and every server-graded feature works on day one. The only front-end that's course-specific is the chapter/arena wiring and the textbook + game files — which we author fresh.

One thing I'll confirm with you at build kickoff

To seed the new course's question banks, final exam, and 7 paper definitions on the server, I'll need either (a) your instructor/admin login to that Supabase platform so I author content through the app's built-in exam/bank editors, or (b) direct project access (service key / SQL) to bulk-load them. Either works — the front-end (shell, textbook, all 13 arenas) I can build regardless; this only gates the server-side answer vaults.

04

The digital textbook — "a very interesting read"

Not a dry summary of the articles. Each chapter is written as an essay that opens on a scene, then pulls the economics out of it — the way a great lecture does. Every chapter follows the same seven-beat template so the book feels like one authored work, not thirteen stitched handouts.

Per-chapter template

The seven beats

  1. Cold open
    A specific moment from the film — WALL·E alone on a trash-planet; Gekko's "greed is good" — that dramatizes the problem.
  2. The economic idea
    The core concept in plain language, with an interactive figure (payoff matrix, supply/demand, decision tree — same figure engine StrategyArena uses).
  3. The reading, unpacked
    The actual argument of Hardin / Coase / Akerlof / Becker — what they claimed, why it mattered, what the math or model says.
  4. The film as evidence
    Where the movie gets the economics right, where it dramatizes, where it's wrong — the critical-thinking core of the course.
  5. Debate & counterpoint
    The other side — Ostrom vs Hardin, Friedman vs Reinhardt — so students see live disagreement, not settled dogma.
  6. Try it & test it
    Inline links to the chapter's Arena and Quiz.
  7. Your paper
    Key terms, 3–4 paper prompts tied to the reading, and a Chicago-citation reminder — feeding the graded paper.
Voice & craft

What makes it a good read

  • Narrative hooks — each chapter earns its first sentence. Stalag 17 opens on cigarettes changing hands; It's a Wonderful Life opens mid bank-run.
  • Real economics, honestly stated — models and results are correct and cited, not hand-waved. This is a 300-level course.
  • Sidebars — "The Money Behind the Scene," "What the Director Got Wrong," short bios (who was Ronald Coase?).
  • Interactive figures — the same in-page diagram widgets StrategyArena renders (matrices, trees, curves) so concepts move, not just sit.
  • Reading bridges — pull-quotes from the assigned articles with a one-line gloss, so the chapter and the PDF reinforce each other.
  • Free vs gated — Ch 0–2 free to preview (matches StrategyArena's free-chapter model); rest unlock on enrollment.
Scope estimate

Roughly 2,500–3,500 words per chapter with 1–2 interactive figures each — a genuine digital textbook of ~35–45k words, written from the assigned readings. Delivered as econfilm-curriculum.html with #chapter-N anchors, exactly like StrategyArena's.

05

The 13 interactive arenas

One playable simulation per topic — you against AI agents, exactly like StrategyArena's games. Each teaches its chapter's model by making the student live it, logs a score to the backend, and counts toward the arena progress track. Below: the mechanic and the learning payoff for each.

Commons Arena

commons
Ch 1 · WALL·E · Hardin & Ostrom
You and 3 AI herders share one pasture (or fishery). Each round you choose how hard to graze. Overuse collapses the resource for everyone — then you unlock Ostrom's tools (quotas, monitoring, sanctions) and watch cooperation re-emerge.
Payoff: feel the free-rider trap, then see why Ostrom beat Hardin's "only markets or Leviathan" dichotomy.

Coase Court

coase
Ch 2 · A Civil Action · Coase
A tannery pollutes; a downstream town is harmed. You set who holds the property right, then let the two parties bargain. Toggle transaction costs from low to high and watch the efficient outcome appear — or fail to.
Payoff: the Coase Theorem in your hands — rights allocation doesn't change efficiency when bargaining is cheap, but does when it isn't.

Deterrence Arena

deterrence
Ch 3 · Dr. Strangelove · Aumann
A repeated nuclear standoff. Build (or bluff) a doomsday device, choose escalate/hold each round against an AI rival. One-shot play tempts defection; repetition and credible commitment sustain an uneasy peace.
Payoff: Aumann's "War and Peace" — why repeated games make cooperation rational and deterrence stable.

Lemons Market

lemons
Ch 4 · Intolerable Cruelty · Akerlof
You buy used cars from AI sellers who know quality you don't. Adverse selection drives good cars out until only lemons remain and the market unravels — then add warranties and signals and watch it heal.
Payoff: Akerlof's market-for-lemons collapse, and how signaling rebuilds trust.

POW Exchange

pow
Ch 5 · Stalag 17 · Radford
Run a POW-camp economy where cigarettes are money. Trade rations, weather a Red Cross parcel glut (inflation) and a drought (deflation), and watch Gresham's Law bite as prisoners smoke the good cigarettes.
Payoff: money emerges spontaneously; supply shocks move the price level — Radford's classic made playable.

Yellow Brick Road

gold-standard
Ch 6 · The Wizard of Oz · Rockoff
Set the money standard for 1890s America — gold only, or free silver. Watch deflation crush indebted farmers (Dorothy's Kansas) while creditors gain, and replay the 1896 Bryan "Cross of Gold" moment.
Payoff: decode Rockoff's allegory and see how the money standard redistributes between debtors and creditors.

Bank Run!

bank-run
Ch 7 · It's a Wonderful Life · Silber
You're George Bailey. A rumor spreads and depositors line up. Choose your moves — pay out, suspend, plead, invoke deposit insurance, ride out the bank holiday — before the Building & Loan runs dry.
Payoff: Diamond–Dybvig fragility, and why FDR's 1933 bank holiday (Silber) actually worked.

Gold Fetters Lab

depression
Ch 8 · The Grapes of Wrath · Bernanke & Romer
Steer a 1930s central bank on the gold standard. Defend the peg or abandon it; set rates; watch money supply, prices, and output. Countries that left gold early (you can, too) recover faster.
Payoff: Bernanke's "golden fetters" thesis — the monetary mechanics behind the Depression and the Joads' road west.

The Boardroom

csr
Ch 9 · Wall Street · Friedman vs Reinhardt
You're the CEO. Each quarter, decide on layoffs, pollution controls, and social initiatives. A pure-Friedman path maximizes short-run profit; strategic CSR sometimes pays more. Gekko (AI) is always watching the stock.
Payoff: the live Friedman-vs-Reinhardt debate — when "the business of business is business," and when CSR is actually profitable.

Subprime Desk

subprime
Ch 10 · The Big Short · Bernanke & Ariely
Trade the 2007 housing bubble. Package or short CDOs as home prices climb and ratings stay rosy; time your bet against the crash. A "fudge-factor" dial models how small dishonesties compound (Mazar–Ariely).
Payoff: the mechanics of the crisis Bernanke testified on, plus the behavioral roots of fraud.

Rational Criminal

crime
Ch 11 · Blow · Becker & Friedman
Set enforcement: probability of arrest × severity of punishment. An AI "offender" weighs expected cost against reward and responds at the margin. Then flip on prohibition and watch the black-market price explode.
Payoff: Becker's crime-as-choice model and Friedman's economic case against the drug war.

Deterrence Data Lab

capital
Ch 12 · Dead Man Walking · Mocan & Levitt
A regression sandbox: adjust execution rates across simulated states and see whether murder rates respond — then run Levitt's four real factors behind the 1990s crime drop and see how much capital punishment actually explains.
Payoff: reading empirical economics critically — correlation, causation, and the limits of the deterrence claim.

Bargaining Line

unions
Ch 13 · Norma Rae · Whaples
Union vs mill. Bargain over wages and the length of the working day; call a strike or settle; a monopsony employer pushes back. Push too hard and the plant closes; too soft and workers stay exploited.
Payoff: bargaining power, monopsony, and how the 8-hour day (Whaples) was actually won.
06

Quizzes & the final exam

All server-graded through the shared backend — questions are generated per-student and correct answers never travel to the browser, the same integrity model StrategyArena uses.

Practice & chapter quizzes

  • Practice: unlimited server-graded items per chapter — instant feedback, no grade pressure.
  • Graded quiz: one short quiz per chapter (≈5–8 items), taken once, answers revealed only after the deadline via "Review missed questions."
  • Item types: concept MCQs on the reading + film-application questions ("Which scene illustrates adverse selection?") + a few numeric items (deadweight loss, expected punishment).
  • Bank size: ~12–18 authored items per chapter so no two students see the same set.
Grade weight

Quizzes are formative by default (0% of the official grade, since your syllabus grades papers + final). One toggle makes them count if you ever want them to — the platform supports per-course quiz weight.

The comprehensive final — 30%

  • Server-graded exam assembled from all 13 chapters, matching your "comprehensive final" worth 30%.
  • Mix of concept MCQs, film-identification, short numeric, and (optionally) 1–2 written long-answers graded with the instructor's AI-assisted rubric tool.
  • Single attempt, timed, proctored via mn-proctor.js snapshots; per-student accommodation overrides for SSD extended time.
  • Given on the BU Brain final-exam schedule; no makeup, per your policy — enforced by the deadline engine.
Your syllabus, encoded

No midterm exists in your course, so the platform's "midterm" weight is set to 0 and only the final carries exam weight.
